God packs are what people clip and share, but they're a rounding error in a collection: just 1.1% of our star-or-better cards came from one. In newer sets the quiet hero is the 6-card pack's bonus slot, which produced 59% of all our shinies. Normal packs in those sets can't contain a shiny at all.

Chapter 4Bad-luck protection

Twelve bad packs, then a guaranteed ex

The July 2026 update announced a guarantee for unlucky streaks (gacha players call this a "pity" system), but never said what triggers it. Here is the rule. The game keeps a hidden count for each set. Every pack whose best card is 3-diamond or lower adds one. Any 4-diamond or better resets it to zero. Hit 12 in a row, and your next pack is guaranteed a 4-diamond or better as its last card.

The count is shared by every pack in that set. We read the count back from every account after every pack: 26,934 of 26,935 followed this rule exactly.

The counter, as a board game
Each square is a count. Arrows show how often a pack moved it forward; ↺ shows how often a hit sent it back to zero.
The only three trainers who reached 12
The pack that took each count from 11 to 12, and what happened next. Hover the cards.

It's real, but it rarely kicks in. About 1 pack in 4 has a 4-diamond or better anyway, so twelve misses in a row is uncommon. Only 3 of our 1,500 accounts got there, all in Genetic Apex. Your odds don't quietly improve as the count climbs, either. Packs 1 to 11 use the normal odds, and the game only swaps the table at 12. Even if you put 100 packs into one set, you only have about a 60% chance of ever seeing a pity pack.

How we know
Observed
We read every account's hidden counters back after every pack: in 26,934 of 26,935 packs the count moved exactly as the rule says. Two accounts reached 12 and their next pack in that set used the guarantee table; a third reached 12 and never opened that set again.
Seen in the app
The guarantee tables appear in the published odds data at 0.000%: they're never rolled, only swapped in at 12.
Limits
Only two real pity packs, both in Genetic Apex, so we can't say much about what a pity pack holds beyond "a 4-diamond or better".

Chapter 6Myth check

What players believe, tested

Most pull superstitions are easy to test with 60,000 packs. Pick one to see the evidence. Most found no effect at all: as far as 60,000 packs can tell, the server rolls every pack from the same odds, whoever you are and whenever you open it. Two turned out to be true. Wonder Pick is in here too.

Chapter 7Which pack should you open?

Same odds for two years. Bigger sets.

Every normal pack, from Genetic Apex to the newest set, uses the same rarity odds for cards 4 and 5. A crown is about 0.2% a pack everywhere. What changed is how many cards share those odds. Newer sets are bigger, so any one card you're chasing is harder to get.

Chapter 8Under the hood

What happens when you tap "Open"

We read the Android app's code (version 1.7.5) and studied every reply the game's servers sent our accounts. Here is the short version of how a pack is actually opened, followed by the best of what we found along the way.

Opening a pack, step by step

The anti-cheat report that pretends to be your favourite energy

One message the app sends looks like a list of favourite energy types: colorless, grass, fire and so on, each true or false. They aren't preferences. Each flag reports a tampering check, such as a rooted phone, an emulator or a modified app, under a harmless-sounding name so it blends in. There were nine checks and ten types, so Metal is a decoy that's never set.

The "energy" field and what it really reports
Decoded from the app's network message definitions

Part of the server is written in Go, and there's no "your server"

Before you buy a pack, the server hands your phone a small token to send back. We decoded 400 of them: each one is a Go-language data blob that quietly identifies which server process made it. We saw 23 different processes, and a single account's tokens came from as many as 8 of them. So at least this part of the server isn't tied to one machine per player; your requests can be served by different processes from one moment to the next.

MethodHow we know what we know

Where the numbers come from

Three sources, cross-checked against each other: purchase receipts from 1,500 accounts, the odds the game publishes in-app, and a static read of the Android client.

The data

  • Pack receipts. Every pack purchase returns the cards and the odds table the server rolled. We logged 60,689 packs and 302,600 cards. A few hundred six-card packs went unlogged. That's why the fleet shows slightly fewer bonus packs than the odds predict (1,656 vs about 1,742).
  • Account readbacks. After every action, each account's balances were read back: pack stamina, hourglasses, Pack Points, XP, level and the hidden pity counters. We used those readings to rebuild the economy and the pity rules.
  • Published odds. The in-app odds screen for each of 28 packs, captured as structured data: 15,179 per-card probabilities across every table and slot.
  • The client. Pokémon TCG Pocket 1.7.5 for Android, decompiled: network message definitions, configuration assets and master data. Findings marked Confirmed are visible in code or data. Inferred findings are strongly implied. Speculative findings are labelled.
  • The run. One week, 1,500 accounts. Accounts were created and played by automation, which cleared beginner content, claimed rewards and opened every pack it could afford.

How we tested

  • Against the published odds, not against each other. Every expectation comes from the game's own odds tables for the exact packs opened. "Luck 1.00×" means exactly as advertised.
  • Correcting for many tests. When we scanned 421 odds cells, 3,240 cards or 163 hour-by-day cells, we corrected p-values (Holm or Benjamini–Hochberg). A single scary-looking cell is expected by chance and isn't reported as a finding.
  • Luck scores. Each account's exact sequence of packs was replayed 20,000 times with the published odds. Its luck percentile is the share of replays that came out less lucky. Only ◆◆◆◆-or-better outcomes, god packs and bonus cards count.
  • Wonder Pick weighting. We compared the likelihood of the 797 observed picks under a fair 1-in-5 model and under a model that applies the rarity weights found in the client, with a fitted exponent in between.

Biases to keep in mind

  • The automation chose which pack to open, to chase missing cards. That skews which packs were opened, but not what came out of them.
  • Wonder Pick feeds were chosen because they contained a wanted card. The server's pick among the five wasn't affected by that.
  • Accounts played like a very efficient new player for one week. Long-term, paying or late-game behaviour isn't covered, and dupe protection near a complete collection is untested.
  • No pack launched during the run, so launch-day luck is untested.
